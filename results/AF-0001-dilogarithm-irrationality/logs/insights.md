# Insights

The ideas that made the proof work. For each: the idea, why it works, where it is used in
the manuscript, and its limits. Scope: the ideas of this contribution only.

## Enlarged families with value-dependent parameters

* **Idea.** The dimension h = Hn and the pole range K = Cn grow faster than the numerator length A = Bn, with (H, B, C) chosen per value (Table 1).
* **Mechanism.** The content gained from the extra poles outweighs the loss in the archimedean estimate for these parameters; for 2/9 the weight has no zeros at all.
* **Used in.** Section 1.2, Table 1.
* **Scope and limits.** Margins are small for 2/9; the parameters were found numerically.

## Reflection at the prime dividing the denominator

* **Idea.** A reflection formula for geometric moments turns the factors λ^{−l} into a gain of order h² at the prime Q dividing b.
* **Mechanism.** Lemma 3.4 rewrites the moments so that powers of Q divide the reflected terms.
* **Used in.** Lemma 3.4, Proposition 3.5.
* **Scope and limits.** Specific to λ = ±2/b.

## Finite normalised sums instead of p-adic integration

* **Idea.** At ordinary primes the functional is distributed over residue classes with finite normalised geometric sums and an affine change of variable carrying the indeterminate.
* **Mechanism.** Each class contributes a block of computable valuation; two Cauchy–Binet expansions give the jet and the pole bounds.
* **Used in.** Proposition 4.3, Lemma 4.4, Propositions 4.5–4.6.
* **Scope and limits.** Tested only through consequences on exact data.

## Two rays for negative arguments

* **Idea.** For λ < 0 the alternating sign is kept and a residue identity on a contour made of two rays replaces the sum by an integral with an exponentially decaying kernel.
* **Mechanism.** The energy comparison then applies to measures on the two rays.
* **Used in.** Section 8.
* **Scope and limits.** Constants not explicit.
